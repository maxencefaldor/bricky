# The five-story pagoda of Tō-ji, Kyoto

- About 55 m tall (54.8 m): the tallest wooden tower in Japan.
- First built in the 9th century, then destroyed and rebuilt four times.
  The present pagoda was rebuilt in the 1640s on the orders of the third Tokugawa shogun, Iemitsu.
- Five stories, each with its own roof.
  Its pillars stand on stone foundations around a central column, and four Buddhas face the four directions at ground level inside.
- Tō-ji was founded in 796 and is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto".

Like other Japanese pagodas, each roof has deep, upturned eaves, the stories narrow toward the top, and a tall metal finial with stacked rings crowns the roof.
